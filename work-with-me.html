Three ways to work together

The right engagement depends on whether you need a decision, a defined implementation or ongoing senior engineering ownership. The problem and outcome come before the format.

  • 01 / Production Risk & Delivery Sprint
    For one important technical decision, production risk or integration problem. Five business days, up to 15 hours, with written findings, prioritised next steps and a handover. A small prototype may fit when agreed in scope.
  • 02 / Scoped Engineering Delivery
    For a clear product improvement, integration, reliability fix, modernisation or practical AI capability. Agree on the result, acceptance criteria, deliverables and fee before implementation.
  • 03 / Embedded Senior Engineering Partner
    For teams needing hands-on architecture, technical leadership and engineering delivery over an agreed period. Define capacity, responsibilities, communication and review points in advance.

Early product builds and AI engineering can fit within scoped or embedded engagements. I also consider selected permanent senior roles; see role fit.

A practical starting point

When the right scope is not yet clear, begin with a Production Risk & Delivery Sprint. In five business days, I inspect the evidence, map the workflow and system boundaries, and make the risks and trade-offs explicit.

  • Product outcome and user workflow
  • System boundaries and data flow
  • Correctness and operational risk map
  • Failure modes, dependencies and verification points
  • Technical plan and production path
  • One bounded implementation or prototype, when useful
  • 60-minute handoff

The Decision-to-Delivery Method

One approach, adapted to the level of responsibility the project needs.

  1. Define. Clarify the user or business outcome, what is at stake and how a useful result will be assessed.
  2. Decide. Examine the relevant evidence, options, constraints and risks before committing to a technical direction.
  3. Deliver. Implement and test the agreed scope, or finish with a documented action plan when the engagement is advisory.
  4. Prove. Review the work against agreed criteria, document remaining risks and hand over decisions and deliverables.

Before any engagement begins, we agree on scope, fees, communication and deliverables in writing. Changes to scope are discussed before additional work. There is no obligation to continue beyond the agreed engagement.
