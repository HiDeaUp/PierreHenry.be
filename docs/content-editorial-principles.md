# Content editorial principles

## Purpose

Build long-term trust in Pierre-Henry Soria as a senior technology professional who applies sound engineering judgement, builds useful products and solves difficult product and technology problems.

Content should support reputation, valuable relationships, high-quality opportunities, customer conversations, speaking and collaboration, and the discovery of repeated problems that may justify a product. Short-term engagement is secondary.

## Editing a draft or idea

When working from a draft, note, transcript or observation:

1. Identify the strongest useful idea.
2. Preserve Pierre-Henry's real opinion and uncertainty.
3. Remove repetition and claims that the evidence does not support.
4. Improve the opening so the problem and relevance are clear.
5. Add a concrete example, decision or trade-off where available.
6. Connect the technical choice to its consequence when that improves the explanation.
7. Keep the ending natural. Do not add a sales pitch by default.

Show experience through decisions, implementation details and outcomes. Do not claim expertise when the content can demonstrate it.

## Audience

Some work can be deeply technical for software engineers and architects. Other work should help founders, CTOs, CEOs and product leaders understand the decision and its consequences without removing the technical substance.

Useful consequences include:

- money;
- risk;
- delivery speed;
- customer experience;
- reliability;
- growth;
- product quality;
- technical debt;
- security;
- scalability;
- team effectiveness.

Do not force a business consequence into a post when the technical detail is useful on its own.

## Topic mix

Use a natural mix of:

- software engineering and architecture;
- engineering leadership;
- AI and AI-assisted engineering;
- health software;
- product development;
- startups and entrepreneurship;
- lessons from building products;
- technical decision-making;
- New Zealand technology;
- current building, testing and learning.

Do not make every topic about AI or one industry.

## Voice

Write in a simple, direct, concise, confident and practical style. Keep the person visible behind the technical work.

Avoid corporate marketing language, motivational-influencer language, unsupported certainty, ornamental vocabulary and generic AI phrasing. Do not use em dashes.

Avoid these expressions:

- firstly, secondly and lastly;
- endless and steadily;
- shape and sharp;
- forefront;
- unleash;
- effortless;
- embrace and embark;
- delve;
- streamline and seamless;
- unveil and unmask;
- game-changer;
- evolving;
- transformative experience;
- let's dive;
- cutting-edge technology;
- digital age;
- fast-paced;
- unlock and supercharge;
- indulge.

## Claims

- Do not invent stories, customers, numbers, achievements or experiences.
- Separate verified experience from a target market or future direction.
- Keep health software as part of the wider engineering identity.
- Describe security through the actual security-advisory automation work unless broader security-engineering evidence is available.
- Do not present Pierre-Henry as a generic freelance developer, cheap agency, unsupported specialist, AI personality or business coach.
- Preserve existing work unless changing it produces a specific improvement in trust, credibility, usefulness, memorability, authority, relationships, opportunities, customer interest or long-term commercial value.
