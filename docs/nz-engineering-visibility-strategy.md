# New Zealand senior engineering visibility strategy

## Objective

Make Pierre-Henry discoverable and credible when a recruiter, founder, technical leader, potential customer or collaborator needs a senior software engineer, technology builder, technical adviser or software architect in New Zealand.

The intended conclusion is:

> Pierre-Henry turns product ideas into shipped, correctness-critical software and uses AI-native engineering workflows to do it faster without dropping engineering controls.

Wellington is the primary market. Australia and remote roles remain in scope.

The long-term objective is broader than securing a role. The professional presence should create senior career opportunities, valuable customer conversations, speaking and collaboration invitations, and evidence about repeated problems that could justify a scalable product.

1. End-to-end product ownership from problem definition to production software.
2. Correctness-critical systems in regulated, compliance-heavy and operationally demanding environments.
3. AI-native workflows using agent orchestration, Model Context Protocol tools and reusable skills.
4. Unusual communication leverage through open source, a programming language, four software-engineering courses and technical publishing.

Banking, food tech, enterprise SaaS and health software are evidence of range. No single industry should become the identity. Health software is a meaningful experience area and a possible New Zealand market angle, but the public evidence does not support presenting Pierre-Henry as a domain-standards specialist.

## Content strategy

Prioritise first-hand, technically specific content:

1. Publish one detailed article about using AI agents to ship correctness-critical software.
2. Publish one production reliability case study when the details can be shared responsibly.
3. Publish one architecture article tied to a recurring decision, not a generic list.
4. Reuse the strongest idea as a LinkedIn post, short video and diagram.
5. Link every derivative format back to the canonical page.

Each article should answer a real question, show what Pierre-Henry decided and explain what evidence would change that decision.

Use two complementary content layers:

- technical depth for engineers and architects;
- decision context for founders, CTOs, CEOs and product leaders.

When relevant, connect the engineering decision to money, risk, delivery speed, customer experience, reliability, growth, product quality, technical debt, security, scalability or team effectiveness. Do not add business language where the technical detail is the useful point.

Use a natural mix of software engineering, architecture, engineering leadership, AI, health software, product development, startups, entrepreneurship, technical decision-making, New Zealand technology and current building or learning. No topic needs to be forced into AI or one industry.

## Problem discovery loop

Content and conversations should also reveal potential products:

1. Publish or discuss a first-hand engineering problem.
2. Record who experiences it, how often and what consequence it creates.
3. Separate an individual implementation issue from a repeated market problem.
4. Test whether companies already spend money or staff time addressing it.
5. Offer advice or implementation only when it helps validate the problem responsibly.
6. Consider a scalable product only after repeated evidence, not from engagement alone.

Review monthly:

- impressions and clicks for target role and location queries;
- indexed pages and crawl issues;

- relevant recruiter, founder and engineering-leader conversations;
- qualified customer and technical-advisory conversations;
- introductions, interviews and role opportunities;
- speaking, workshop and collaboration invitations;
- links from relevant technical and professional sources;
- which evidence caused a person to make contact.
- repeated problems with an identifiable buyer, consequence and current workaround.

Follower counts and raw views provide context. They are not the outcome.

## Claim discipline

- State only skills and outcomes that can be supported.
- Keep confidential employer and system details appropriately general.
- Separate experience from target roles.
- Do not imply domain standards expertise that is not present.
- Use health software as one example of regulated product work, not as the main identity.
- Preserve historical pages while keeping the current profile clear and current.

## Decision filter

Before changing the professional presence or publishing content, identify which outcome it improves:

- trust;
- credibility;
- usefulness;
- memorability;
- authority;
- relationships;
- opportunities;
- customer interest;
- long-term commercial value.

If none improves, the change is probably unnecessary.
