# Before, after and evidence risks

No source CV or LinkedIn text was available in the repository. The “before” column therefore uses the exact facts supplied in the positioning brief. The “after” column shows how those facts were reframed in the draft artefacts.

## Source-to-draft comparison

| Before: supplied fact | After: profile wording | Concrete reason |
|---|---|---|
| “Product-first senior software engineer, 12 years.” | “Product-first software engineer with 12 years of experience turning product ideas into shipped, correctness-critical software.” | Preserves the validated experience while leading with the result a hiring reader should remember. |
| “Currently building enterprise automation for a security advisory group at a major bank.” | “Build and ship enterprise automation that turns operational and security processes into working software.” | Moves from employer context to product ownership and shipped output. Measured impact is still required. |

| “Founded my own healthy-food product.” | “Founded and built a healthy-food product, combining product definition, engineering and founder-level ownership.” | Makes food tech relevant to Founding Engineer roles without treating it as a side project. |

## What was deliberately not included

- Any unsupported domain-standard or cross-system data-exchange expertise.
- Any claim that health tech is the main professional identity.
- A direct claim of being faster than colleagues.
- Employer names, job titles, dates, institutions or technologies not supplied or previously validated.
- Product scale, revenue, users, downloads, stars, ratings or performance improvements without evidence.
- Generic self-description that is not tied to evidence.

## Weak points that need evidence

### 1. The current role has responsibility but no result

“Building enterprise automation” is credible context, but it does not yet show impact. Add two or three verified outcomes:

- hours or steps removed from a workflow;
- cycle-time change;
- number and type of users or teams served;
- quality, traceability or control improvement;
- reduction in manual review or rework;
- exact scope owned from idea to production.

### 2. The health-tech role lacks scale and ownership

Doctors, patients and pharmacies establish a meaningful product surface. The profile still needs:

- what Pierre-Henry personally owned;
- the technical stack used in that role;
- whether the work was greenfield, scaling or remediation;
- release, reliability or user outcomes;
- team and product context.

### 3. The New Zealand experience is too vague

This is strategically important for Wellington, but currently contributes almost no proof. Add organisation, role, product, dates, users, stack and one outcome.

### 4. The open-source metric proves endurance, not adoption

More than 11,000 commits over 13 years is strong evidence of authorship and maintenance. Add verified adoption evidence if it exists:

- users or production installations;
- releases and backwards-compatibility work;
- contributors and community ownership;
- stars, downloads or dependent projects;
- difficult architectural changes maintained over time.

### 5. AI-native delivery needs one concrete case study

Agents, MCPs and skills are differentiating only when connected to a shipped result. Add one example covering:

- the product or workflow problem;
- what the agent system handled;
- what Pierre-Henry reviewed or controlled;
- cycle-time, cost or quality change;
- what failed and how the workflow was corrected.

### 6. The target role list is too broad for one application

The base profile can support several roles. Each application should use one primary title and reorder evidence for that role. A Founding Engineer CV should lead with product ownership and breadth. A Principal CV should lead with cross-team technical decisions and system impact. An AI Product Engineer CV should lead with shipped AI-enabled workflows and evaluation.

## Website consistency status

The earlier website draft contained unsupported domain-standard and cross-system data-exchange claims. Those claims did not follow from the experience stated in the brief and are absent from the current public-site changes.

The revised homepage, work-with-me page, CV draft and LinkedIn draft now use the same positioning:

- product and engineering judgement first;
- banking, food tech, health software and enterprise SaaS as evidence of range;
- Wellington as the primary market without implying current residence;
- AI-native delivery described as a controlled engineering workflow;
- no unsupported specialist, standards or data-exchange claims.

Remaining consistency work is operational: publish the website changes, align LinkedIn after authentication, and update the extended profile when its source or administration path is available. Historical work should remain available and unchanged.
