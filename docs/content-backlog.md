## Selection scorecard

Score each idea from 1 to 5 before committing to a full article:

- credibility: direct experience and evidence available;
- market need: a repeated problem for the intended reader;
- differentiation: a perspective more useful than generic summaries;
- technical depth: enough substance to show architecture and trade-offs;
- professional relevance: proximity to roles Pierre-Henry wants;
- business consequence: a clear connection to money, risk, speed, customers, reliability, growth, product quality, technical debt, security, scalability or team effectiveness;
- problem-discovery value: useful evidence about a repeated problem, buyer or current workaround;
- visual potential: one standalone asset can explain the core idea.

Do not publish by score alone. Use the score to make assumptions visible, then apply editorial judgement.

## Required article package

Every substantial article should include:

1. a concrete engineering question;
2. first-hand evidence or an explicit hypothetical boundary;
3. the practical consequence for users, the business, the system or the team when relevant;
4. one original visual when it materially improves the explanation;
5. a concise LinkedIn version;
6. an optional X thread when sequence matters;
7. a small code example only when it improves understanding;
8. potential follow-up questions;
9. a row in the content-performance tracker with unknown analytics left blank.
